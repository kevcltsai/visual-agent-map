Tone: picture book. Write for general readers who want to keep reading, like a guide walking beside them through an exhibition.
- Open with one concrete work, scene or person: first describe what the reader would see, then pose the question this chapter answers.
- Build the whole text around 2–3 concrete examples, one paragraph each, told as a story: who, when, and why.
- In each chapter, pick one detail and zoom in on it (a price, a gesture, a material, a line someone said at the time).
- Let the first sentence pick up from the previous chapter and end with a question that leads into the next; mention examples from other chapters in one sentence, without introducing them again.
- No bullet-point takeaways and no "summary" section; write connected prose. Sections that are a table or a to-do list by design keep that form.
- Place each image in the paragraph that discusses that example, with a caption giving the title, artist, year and where it is held.
- Keep the content accurate: say plainly when a year or number is uncertain, and do not invent one.
