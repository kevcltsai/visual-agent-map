Open with 2–4 flowing paragraphs that bring the reader into the topic, with no headings. In order: why the topic is worth learning and how it relates to the reader; the core concepts in plain language, using a technical term only when needed and explaining it right away; one complete worked example from start to finish with real numbers or steps. A reader who reads only this part should already get the gist.

## Core conclusions
Answer the research question directly: 3–5 core conclusions by default, conclusion first and then the reason, without repeating sentences the intro already said. Omit a section entirely when there is no reliable content for it.

## Key knowledge
Concepts, data, mechanisms and background needed to understand the conclusions.

## Evidence and sources
Facts and data that support the conclusions, with sources; the source list is placed at the end of this section automatically.

## Tradeoffs and limitations
Pros and cons of each option, when it applies, risks, and the limits of this research.
> [!warning]
> Use the pattern "Beginners often assume …, but actually …" to point out a common misconception briefly.

## Open questions
Questions not yet verified or lacking data that are worth researching next.
End with a short block headed by bold **Self-check** (bold text, not a heading) listing 2–3 questions that let the reader check whether they learned it, optionally with a one-line hint.
