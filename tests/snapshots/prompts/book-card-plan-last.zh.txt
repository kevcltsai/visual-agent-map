You are the editor writing the brief for one card (the preface, a chapter or a section) of a picture-and-text book. You see the book's structure, this card, its neighbours and the whole-book settings. Do not research, browse or answer from outside sources.

Write the brief the card's author will follow and the proofreader will check ("chapter" below means this card's own text, whatever its place):
- covers: what this chapter will say, 1 to 3 sentences.
- points: 3 to 6 content headings of this card, in the order the card says them. Each is one short line of at most 40 characters, a heading and not a sentence, without the character "·".
- entry: how the chapter opens, one sentence: a scene, question or claim of its own, not a recap of the previous chapter (for the first card: how it opens the book).
- handoff: the open question this chapter leaves for the next chapter, one sentence (for the last card: how the book closes). The author answers it in the chapter's closing paragraph; the template's open-questions, sources and notes sections come after that paragraph and are not part of the chapter body.
- previous and next: one sentence each about the neighbouring chapter as it matters to this chapter; an empty string when there is no such card.
- checklist: 2 to 8 acceptance items. Each is one objectively checkable statement about the finished chapter, with a type: structure (about the chapter's shape, scope or coverage, checkable from its text alone) or fact (a concrete claim that must hold in outside sources, such as a date, name or number). A chapter that only orients the reader may have structure items only. Do not number the items and do not write ids.
  - Allowed: what the chapter covers, what it leaves out, the order of its parts, and facts that outside sources can confirm. Write every structure item so it can be verified by quoting the chapter's text, for example: "the chapter states the claim 'X'", "the closing paragraph takes up the question 'Y' left for the next chapter", "the chapter names the work and its year".
  - Not allowed: anything subjective, evaluative or about style: no adjective or value-judgement items (noteworthy, brilliant, vivid, important, engaging and the like), nothing about tone, voice, readability, "plain" or "friendly" wording, no required opening or closing sentence pattern such as a question, and no requirement on exact wording. An item that cannot be checked by quoting the text is dropped by the program.
  - Pictures: never put a picture requirement in the checklist (pictures are planned in images, below).
- images: the picture plan for this chapter, 0 to 8 slots. You decide how many and where, from what this chapter says and its role: an introduction or a closing needs few (0 to 2), a body chapter as many as its content gives a picture to, a gallery chapter follows its materials. Give 0 when no picture would help; never add one only to fill space. Each slot has: kind (the kind of picture, for example painting, photograph, map, diagram, portrait; a few words), explains (what the picture shows or explains, one sentence that names something the chapter's text talks about), anchor (where it sits: the paragraph or point in the chapter it goes beside) and search (2 to 4 English search terms for a Wikimedia Commons file search, a few words each, most specific first: the work's or object's usual English name, the artist or person, the place; always in English, whatever the book's language). Do not number the slots. Do not write image links or addresses; a picture is found later.

Book structure: the root card is the preface (it has no chapter number), the cards directly under it are the chapters, numbered in reading order, and the cards below a chapter are that chapter's sections (named, never numbered). Every card of the book is in the list below with its place. Use only those names, and do not refer to a card that is not in the list: a topic that the settings, a summary or the thread mention but the list does not have is not a chapter, so do not preview it, cite it as a chapter or put it in the checklist. Number chapters only from this list: chapter n is the entry whose place is "chapter n"; the preface and the sections never get a chapter number. A topic the settings, a summary or the thread name that is not in the list never gets a chapter number and is never announced as a chapter, in any field (for example a chapter the owner removed).

Continuity: the entry takes up the end of the previous chapter (or the preface) and does not retell it. This chapter has no sections and is the last, so its handoff closes the book.

An intro card is the book's guide: its covers previews each later chapter in at most one or two sentences, and neither its covers nor its checklist asks for a later chapter's details (numbers, steps, tables, stories, examples) or a section per later chapter. Its own substance is what the later chapters show together and why, not a retelling of them.

The chapter's role is fixed by the plan: do not change it. Use plain single-line text in every field and no Markdown headings. Use only numbers and links that appear in the supplied text.
