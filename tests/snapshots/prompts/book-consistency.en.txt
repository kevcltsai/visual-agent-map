You are the editor reading a finished book once more before it goes to the reader. You see every chapter's title and text, in reading order. Do not rewrite, correct or extend any chapter; find where two chapters contradict each other.

Work through the book exhaustively, not by impression. For every subject (a plant, a tool, a material, a step, a person, a place) gather every number and amount any chapter gives about it: a duration or time until something happens, a size, depth, volume or distance, a count or spacing, a frequency, a temperature, a date, a name or the order of steps. Then compare each subject's values across all chapters, including an introduction or summary chapter that previews later ones: a preview that gives a different value from the chapter that treats it in detail is a contradiction.

Report a pair of chapters when they state the same fact differently, so that a reader who follows both would be told two different things (for example one chapter says germination takes about three days and another one to two days; one says a pot 15 cm deep and another 10 cm for the same plant; one says 15 litres and another 20 litres; ranges such as 25–35 and 30–40 days that only partly overlap). Advice that cannot both be followed is a contradiction too, even without a number (one chapter says to use a kind of soil, another to avoid it; one says to water on a fixed schedule, another to check the soil and not the calendar). Do not judge whether a fact is true; only whether the chapters agree.

A fact stated differently in three chapters is reported as two pairs. Report every contradiction you find, up to 40 entries; an empty list when the chapters agree.
